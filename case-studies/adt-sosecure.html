Turning an emergency-use app into a daily-use app

How I designed real-time location sharing for ADT's SoSecure while keeping the SOS slider existing users relied on.

At Nagarro, we worked as contractors for ADT, with their business team involved in product decisions and direction. When I joined, SoSecure already existed as ADT's mobile safety app. I designed its new real-time location sharing feature, the in-app chat and a new homepage, working closely with another designer and the engineering team.

Pain points and main goals

Reduce churn

SoSecure lost 94% of users who opened the app on day one and then deleted it.

Improve engagement

The app was mainly used in emergencies, giving people little reason to open it regularly.

Attract new users

Most users were existing ADT customers, mainly from an older demographic.

Based on ADT's project brief.

The challenge

SoSecure gave people different ways to ask for help in an emergency: an SOS slider, SMS chat, video and a hands-free option using a secret phrase, even from a locked screen.

These features were important when something went wrong, but people had little reason to open the app on a normal day. Most users opened it once and then deleted it.

ADT wanted to reach a younger, Millennial audience and turn SoSecure into an app people would use every day. The challenge was to introduce new reasons to use it without losing the safety features existing customers relied on.

From emergency-use to daily-use

The starting point was an app built around emergencies, with the SOS slider at the centre of the homepage.

Through customer archetypes and competitive research, Nagarro and ADT found that the mobile safety market was fragmented, with no clear product positioning. We explored how SoSecure could combine personal safety with features people already used in social apps, such as sharing their location with family and friends.

This meant moving beyond the individual ADT customer and thinking about families and groups of friends as the main users of the app.

The main goals were:

  • Encourage daily use: Give people reasons to open the app beyond emergencies.
  • Make it personal: Put the user and their groups at the centre of the experience.
  • Fit into everyday routines: Make location sharing useful in people's day-to-day lives.

ADT decided to make location sharing the main feature. Our role was to design the experience for this new audience without removing what made SoSecure different: the SOS slider.

Designing the experience

We started by defining three personas to represent the new audience:

Robert

Account holder, 43

A travelling businessman and primary caregiver to his mother.

Lisa

Account holder, 40

A working mum who uses online tools to manage different parts of her life.

Bella

Free account, 17

A teenager who is always on her phone and values privacy and control over her personal data.

As a team of two designers, we worked in parallel on different parts of the experience. We used wireframes to explore ideas, define flows and agree on the main features. The engineering team was involved early in the process.

Homepage

The homepage needed to introduce location sharing while keeping the emergency features easy to access.

Our first concept removed the SOS slider and put groups at the centre of the experience. But user testing showed us how important the slider was to existing users, and ADT wasn't ready to remove it.

We explored different layouts before settling on a balance between the old and new experiences. The slider stayed at the top, alongside the other emergency options (SOS chat and SOS video). The remaining features were organised into two sections: My Safety Kit and My Groups.

In-app chat

With location sharing, we also wanted group members to be able to communicate without leaving the app.

Until then, conversations with ADT agents happened over SMS. I designed an in-app chat for both agent conversations and group chats. The agent chat included quick replies such as “I'm being followed”, while group members could share their location or ask someone to check in.

Design system and accessibility

I proposed creating a design system in Figma to make the work more consistent and easier to maintain. I built it together with the other designer, covering typography, colours, buttons, cards, icons and illustrations.

We also considered accessibility from the start. We checked visual layout, text-to-speech compatibility and colour contrast against ADA standards, which met legal requirements and helped ADT reach customers of all abilities.

Outcomes

  • SoSecure launched with real-time location sharing as its main feature, including groups, spots, check-ins and in-app chat, on iOS and Android. The launch happened after I left the project.
  • The new homepage brought location sharing into everyday view while keeping the slider existing customers relied on.
  • The Figma design system and ADA checks gave the app a shared, accessible foundation.

What I learned

Our first homepage concept went too far. Removing the slider made room for groups, but user testing showed us how much that one component meant to people and to ADT.

When a product changes its purpose, existing users need something they still recognise. Keeping the slider at the top gave them that anchor, and location sharing could grow around it instead of replacing it.
