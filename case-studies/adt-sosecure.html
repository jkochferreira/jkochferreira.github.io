The challenge

SoSecure lost 94% of its users after their first day. People installed it, used it once and deleted it.

ADT wanted to reduce that churn, make the app something people used actively, and reach new users. Most of the existing users were ADT home security customers from an older demographic, and ADT wanted the app to appeal to Millennials.

The product was built entirely around emergencies. It had an SOS slider, SMS chat with an agent, video, and a hands-free secret phrase that worked from a locked screen. Those features mattered on a bad day, but they gave people no reason to open the app on a normal one.

From an SOS button to a family map

We started from the existing SoSecure app and the question of where it fit in the mobile market.

Using customer archetypes and competitive research, we found the mobile safety market was fragmented and not clearly defined. Safety on its own wasn't enough to bring people back. So we positioned the new SoSecure between a lifestyle app and a social network, built around one new feature, real-time location sharing.

That changed who the app was for. Instead of one ADT customer, the users were now whole families, from a man caring for his elderly mother to a teenager who wanted control over her personal data. We defined three personas to keep all of them in view.

We set three goals for the redesign:

  • Make it sticky: people remember and value the apps they use every day.
  • Make it personal: put the user and their people at the centre of the app.
  • Make it part of everyday: weave the app into normal routines, so it is already open when something goes wrong.

Designing the experience

As a team of two designers working in an agile way, we ran several steps at the same time. We used wireframes for early ideation and to define the UX before moving to final designs, focusing first on patterns, flows and features. That was also when the engineering team got actively involved.

The homepage

The homepage had to carry the move from an emergency app to a daily one without losing the existing customers.

In our first concept, we removed the SOS slider and made groups the focus of the screen. User testing showed us the slider was the signature element of the app, and ADT wasn't ready for such a disruptive change. [confirm: how many people took part in testing?]

We kept the slider at the top, with the other emergency options (SOS chat and SOS video) right below it. The rest of the features moved into two blocks, My Safety Kit and My Groups. Existing customers found their emergency tools where they expected them, and new users saw their family's locations straight away.

Real-time location sharing

Location sharing was the feature the whole strategy rested on. We built it around three ideas.

  • Groups: private groups of up to 10 people, who see each other on a shared map.
  • Spots: saved places such as home, school or work. With alerts on, the app sets up a geofence and tells the group when someone arrives or leaves.
  • Check-ins: one tap shares where you are with the group, even somewhere that isn't a saved spot.

People could create as many groups as they wanted for free. Because SoSecure ran on both iOS and Android, families with mixed phones could use one app.

We also designed the details that make groups work over time: joining with a six-character invite code, choosing which members' alerts you get, setting a spot's radius, snoozing group alerts, and transferring group ownership.

In-app chat

Until then, people talked to an ADT agent by SMS. Once groups could share locations, we wanted members to be able to talk to each other too, so we rethought the agent conversation as well and moved it into an in-app chat.

In an emergency, the agent chat offers quick replies such as “I'm being followed” or “I had an accident”. Group chats let members share a pinned location, ask for a check-in or get driving directions.

A design system with accessibility built in

We built a design system in Figma to standardise the work and make it faster: typography, colours, buttons, cards, icons and illustrations.

We wanted SoSecure to be accessible to everyone, which mattered even more for an app with many older users. We checked visual layout, text-to-speech compatibility and colour contrast against ADA standards. That covered ADT's legal requirements and helped it reach customers of all abilities.

What I learned

Our first homepage concept went too far. We removed the SOS slider to make room for groups, and user testing showed us how much that one component meant to people and to ADT.

When a product changes its purpose, existing users need something they still recognise. Keeping the slider at the top gave them that anchor, and the new features could grow around it instead of replacing it.
