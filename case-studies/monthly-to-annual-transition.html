PandaDoc — Monthly to Annual

From one renewal dialog to a broader annual plan strategy

How a first experiment at PandaDoc led to a new approach to moving monthly customers to annual.

Role
Senior Product Designer, design owner for M2A
Team
PM, 4 engineers, Data, Research and CRM
Services
Product design, experiment design, UX research, design systems
Industry
B2B SaaS, document workflow
Timeline
September 2025 – January 2026

I joined the monthly-to-annual work after a first experiment showed that some customers would switch, but that a single message wasn't enough. I used those learnings to define a broader strategy, then designed and tested touchpoints from plan comparison to trial checkout.

The first three come from the trial checkout tooltip test after 14 days, compared with a savings card. The churn figure is company-wide, FY24 to FY25 (year to date).

The challenge

Monthly customers churned six times faster than annual ones (5.18% a month vs 0.88%), and only 0.3% of Business Monthly accounts switched to annual each month. The goal was to reduce churn in the 2–50 seat segment and improve net dollar retention (NDR).

Annual was also hard to find. The plan comparison page didn't mention it, the billing page link converted at 0.5%, and 60% of trials that started on annual switched to monthly before paying.

From experiment to strategy

The first experiment, designed by another designer, showed Business Monthly admins a dialog once, 14 days before renewal, highlighting the annual savings without an extra discount.

It reached our 1% conversion target, up from 0.3%, but only 1.75% clicked. As a modal, it was easy to dismiss without reading. Of the 977 customers who declined, 30.6% preferred monthly flexibility, 24.3% wanted to switch later and 22.8% found it too expensive.

Showing the savings once wasn't enough. We needed to reach the right customers at the right time, with a relevant offer and more than one chance to switch.

I designed the strategy around three areas: segmentation (engaged customers, growing teams, power users and accounts at risk), timing (milestones and behaviours instead of a single message) and offers (extra discounts, annual-only perks and bundles).

I shared my proposals in Figma, and together with the PM and engineering manager we chose which touchpoints to deliver.

Designing the experience

I first mapped the customer's first year and the ways the product could talk to customers. This set when to reach people, in which format, and how loud each message should be: subtle nudges throughout, more prominent messages at milestones.

Plan comparison

I added a monthly/annual toggle to each plan card and changed "Select plan" to Upgrade and Downgrade. An earlier study had shown that toggles could confuse people, so we tested the design in Maze before building: 93–97% task success with 30 participants, and 67% preferred the toggle inside the card. It became the standard for that page.

Checkout and billing

At checkout, the saving moved inside the plan card, where it couldn't be missed or compete with the price. On the billing page, a more prominent savings component got fewer clicks, but more of them converted.

Trial checkout

Instead of another message for everyone, I designed a tooltip that appears only when someone selects a monthly plan. It beat a persistent savings card in a 50/50 test and was rolled out to all trials.

Customer milestones

A quarter of the people who declined said they would switch later. At three months, a time-limited offer ran across an email, an in-app dialog and a homepage banner, with rules so the messages didn't stack.

A side project: a system for nudges

Most nudge ideas kept ending up as a banner or a modal. Together with another product designer, I mapped the nudge patterns in the product and wrote guidelines for which one to use, based on what triggers the message, from tooltips and toasts to callouts and email.

The biggest gap was a contextual message between a tooltip and a dialog. With the design system team, we chose a new callout component over a variant of the existing Alert, because nudges shouldn't look like system alerts. The callout has five variants (Default, Highlight, Upsell, AI and Reminder) and was used by the wider team.

Outcomes

  • The trial checkout tooltip was rolled out to all trials.
  • Monthly-to-annual was named the highest-impact churn initiative of FY25. Average churn dropped by about 8%, a company-wide result.
  • The toggle became the standard on the plan comparison page, and the tooltip and callout became reusable patterns.

What users said

On the new plan comparison page (Maze study, 30 participants, October 2025):

“Changing to annual was obvious: one quick toggle.”
“I liked seeing both options without leaving the page.”
“You save money yearly: it's clear right there.”
“It took only a few seconds, everything was clear.”

On monthly and annual plans (customer interviews from other PandaDoc studies, [confirm dates]):

“We've always used the monthly one. But you do have, like, a big discount on the annual one… I've always thought of doing that, but I never did that.”
“I'm just kind of leaving it on a month to month. Just kind of seeing…”
“I'd probably pay the yearly for this if it had the integrations…”

What I learned

The first experiment was most useful for what it revealed. Low engagement and customer feedback moved us from a single dialog to the whole customer journey, and reminded me that the right moment and message matter more than pushing everyone to annual.
