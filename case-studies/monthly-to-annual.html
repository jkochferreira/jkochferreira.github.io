PandaDoc — Monthly to Annual

From one renewal dialog to a broader annual plan strategy

How a first experiment at PandaDoc led to a new approach to moving monthly customers to annual.

Role
Senior Product Designer, design owner for M2A
Scope
Product design, experiment design, UX research, design systems
Timeline
September 2025 – January 2026

I joined PandaDoc's Monthly-to-Annual initiative in September 2025. By then, the team had already tested a modal offering annual savings, shown once during the renewal period. We learned that some customers were willing to switch, but the modal reached very few of them.

I used those learnings to define a broader strategy, then designed and tested different nudges across the customer journey, from the plan comparison page and checkout to key customer milestones.

The challenge

Monthly customers churned six times faster than annual customers, and very few switched to annual on their own.

The business goal was to reduce churn among teams with 2–50 seats. Moving more monthly customers to annual plans could help improve net dollar retention (NDR), but the product made it difficult for customers to explore and compare their options.

Annual plans weren't mentioned on the plan comparison page, the link on the billing page had a conversion rate of just 0.5%, and 60% of trials that started with an annual plan switched to monthly before paying.

We needed to make annual plans easier to find and compare, and understand when and how to encourage customers to switch.

From experiment to strategy

The first experiment, designed by another designer, showed Business Monthly admins a dialog once, 14 days before renewal, highlighting the annual savings without an extra discount.

It reached our conversion target, but only 1.75% of people clicked. The learnings mattered most. Reaching the target with a single modal showed there was room to invest more in moving customers to annual. And the 977 customers who declined told us why: most preferred the flexibility of monthly plans, wanted to switch later, or found annual too expensive.

One message at renewal wasn't enough. I designed the strategy around three areas:

Segmentation

Focus on engaged customers, growing teams, power users and accounts at risk of churning.

Timing

Use customer milestones and behaviours, such as team growth or add-on purchases, instead of relying on a single message.

Offers

Explore extra discounts, annual-only perks and bundles based on customer needs.

I shared my proposals with the PM and engineering manager, and together we decided which ideas to take forward.

Designing the experience

I started by mapping the customer's first year: the milestones where an offer to switch could feel timely rather than pushy, and the notification cadence around each one. How many reminders, on which pages, with what escalating or de-escalating prominence, and through which channels: in the product, from subtle nudges to modal dialogs, or outside it, through email and push notifications.

First drafts

I played with both timing and format: subtle, contextual nudges throughout the year, and more prominent messages at milestones. I drafted several combinations, iterating on form and placement before scaling: persistent banners, inline cards and prompts beside checkout instead of modals, and no offers during critical workflows, such as editing a document.

Final designs

Plan comparison

I added a monthly/annual toggle to each plan card and changed "Manage current plan" to "Save 25%, Switch to annual". An earlier study had shown that toggles could confuse people, so we tested the design in Maze before building: 93–97% task success with 30 participants, and 67% preferred the toggle inside the card. It became the standard for that page.

The nudge in the card

The new card is a behavioural design nudge that influences the customer's decision in a positive way. It puts the annual option and its saving in front of them at the moment they look at their plan, while leaving the choice to them.

What participants said in the Maze study:

“Changing to annual was obvious: one quick toggle.”
“I liked seeing both options without leaving the page.”
“You save money yearly: it's clear right there.”
“It took only a few seconds, everything was clear.”

Permanent nudges

These nudges are contextual: small, non-blocking messages placed next to the decision they relate to, so annual stays visible without interrupting anyone.

Billing page

On the billing page, the savings component became more prominent and showed each customer's own saving, with a direct way to switch. It got fewer clicks, but more of them converted.

Checkout

At checkout, we ran an A/B test with trial accounts. Accounts were split 50/50, and each group saw a different nudge: a savings card on the annual plan card, showing the amount and the % discount, or a tooltip showing the saving as months saved. Each variant reached at least 3,500 accounts, with no control group because of the sample size.

Customer milestones

For customers who wanted to switch later, a time-limited offer ran at three months across an email, an in-app dialog and a homepage banner, with rules so the messages didn't stack.

Side project

A system for nudges

While working on M2A, I noticed that most nudge ideas relied on banners or modals, with no shared guidance on which pattern to use for each message.

Together with another product designer, I reviewed the existing patterns and created guidelines to help teams choose the right one. We grouped nudges by trigger (action, signal, behaviour, time or static) and by whether they were contextual or non-contextual.

The guidelines covered when to use each pattern, how to write the message and its strengths and limitations. They also included practical rules, such as how long a toast should stay on screen and whether a dismissed nudge could appear again.

The callout nudge

One gap stood out: we needed a contextual message that sat between a tooltip and a dialog.

Together with the design system team, we explored whether to extend the existing Alert component or create a new one. We chose a separate callout because alerts need attention, while nudges should guide users without interrupting their work.

I worked with the team on its structure and visual details. The component supports five variants: Default, Highlight, Upsell, AI and Reminder, with different sizes and inverted versions. It was added to the design system for the wider team to use.

Outcomes

  • The plan comparison page made it easier to compare monthly and annual plans.
  • The checkout tooltip was rolled out to all trial accounts.
  • The new callout component and nudge guidelines gave other teams patterns they could reuse.
  • The M2A initiative was named the highest-impact churn initiative of FY25.

What I learned

The first experiment was most useful for what it revealed. Low engagement and customer feedback moved us from a single dialog to the whole customer journey, and reminded me that the right moment and message matter more than pushing everyone to annual.
